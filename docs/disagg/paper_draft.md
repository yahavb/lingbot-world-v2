# [SYSTEM]: Role-Disaggregated Serving for Two-Stage Generative Pipelines (Operational Systems)

## Abstract

Generative models for interactive video, world simulation for robotics and long-form audio share one pipeline:

- a diffusion transformer (DiT) denoises latents;
- a learned decoder (a VAE) turns them into pixels or samples.

Serving stacks run both stages co-located on the same accelerators, one after the other. We show that this coupling,
not either stage alone, limits throughput per chip. The cause differs from the one behind prefill/decode
disaggregation in LLM serving, where a compute-bound phase interferes with a memory-bound one. On AWS Trainium3 the
two stages are bound by different resources:

- the DiT by on-chip data movement;
- the decoder by systolic-array utilization.

So they scale differently with chips. A co-located replica runs each stage on the other's chip count, and makes each
stage wait for the other.

We formulate the cost of the coupling and validate it:

- it predicts all 15 measured configurations of a 14B streaming world model within 1.4%;
- it predicts a held-out 16-chip DiT within 2.5%.

We build [SYSTEM] on Amazon EKS. Denoiser and decoder roles are sized, replicated, placed and failed over
independently:

- DRA claims pair each Trainium device with its own EFA;
- NIXL writes each latent from device memory to device memory in 0.17 ms;
- decoders keep per-session causal state, so that one decoder serves many streams.

On LingBot-World v2 and Stable Audio 3 Medium, [SYSTEM]:

- runs 1.37x the frame rate of the best 16-chip co-located configuration;
- serves 720p where a co-located 8-chip replica does not fit;
- serves 3 streams per 1-chip decoder with no slowdown, and 9 streams on 3 decoders;
- matches co-located press-to-photon latency at the median while beating it at p90.

## 1 Introduction

Real-time generative media is becoming a systems workload. Three examples:

- an interactive world model turns a player's or a robot's actions into video, chunk by chunk;
- a policy-training loop runs many such simulations in parallel;
- an audio model renders half an hour of music in seconds.

All three run the same two stages for every unit of output. A DiT denoises a latent chunk; a VAE decodes it.

Serving systems for these models inherit the LLM playbook: tensor and sequence parallelism for the large model, and
the whole pipeline co-located in each replica. That playbook was designed for a pipeline with one dominant stage.
These pipelines have two, and neither is negligible:

- In a 14B world model on 16 Trainium3 chips, the decoder takes 143 ms per chunk next to the DiT's 300 ms.
- In Stable Audio 3 at its 8-step default, decode is 40-53% of the DiT's time.

The natural question is whether to disaggregate them, as LLM serving disaggregates prefill from decode. The LLM
argument does not carry over. Prefill/decode disaggregation exists because a compute-bound phase and a memory-bound
phase interfere on the same hardware.

We profiled both stages on Trainium3 and found neither profile:

- **The DiT's attention blocks are bound by on-chip data movement.** Their DMA queues are busy 82% of the time,
  moving 8.8x the bytes their HBM traffic requires. Their tensor engine reaches 28% of peak.
- **The decoder's convolutions are bound by array utilization.** The tensor engine is busy about 90% of the time,
  yet delivers 44-50% of its peak.

Both stages are far from their roofline ceilings, for different reasons. The consequence is that they scale
differently:

- The world model's DiT keeps gaining from chips, 552 ms per chunk at 4 chips and 299 ms at 16. Its decoder barely
  changes, about 140 ms at 4, 8 and 16 chips.
- The audio model's DiT cannot shard beyond two chips, while its decode scales almost linearly.

Any single chip count is wrong for one of the two stages.

This paper makes four contributions:

1. **A cost model of stage coupling.** From per-stage scaling curves, a co-located replica takes T_d(N) + T_v(N) per
   unit of work, and a disaggregated one max(T_d(n_d), T_v(n_v)). One decoder replica serves about T_d / T_v
   denoiser replicas. The model predicts every measured configuration within 1.4%.
2. **Role disaggregation as an operating-system abstraction.** A pipeline is a set of roles. Each role is sized,
   replicated, placed and failed over independently; the state that crosses roles is explicit. Sizing is a
   deploy-time decision, because steps, resolution and model are fixed per service.
3. **[SYSTEM], an implementation on EKS and Trainium,** built from DRA, EFA, NIXL and per-session decoder state.
4. **An evaluation on two pipelines.** It covers chip budgets, resolutions, denoising steps, N:M replica ratios,
   latency and failures. It reports where disaggregation does not pay, and why.

## 2 Background

**Two-stage pipelines.**

- **LingBot-World v2 is a 14B causal video world model.**
  - Each chunk is 3 latent frames, decoded to 12 video frames.
  - The DiT runs 4 denoising steps with a rolling KV cache (a sink chunk plus a 4-chunk window), then a cache
    commit pass.
  - A causal 3-D convolutional VAE keeps a temporal cache across chunks.
  - Chunks are 480x768, or 720p (a 704x1280 grid).
- **Stable Audio 3 Medium generates long-form audio in 380 s windows.**
  - Each window after the first is inpainted from the previous window's last 60 s, which must be decoded and
    re-encoded first.
  - Its decoder works on independent overlapping chunks.

**Trainium3.**

- A trn3 chip runs 4 logical cores at LNC2.
- Each core has tensor, vector, scalar and GpSimd engines, plus DMA queues that move data between HBM and on-chip
  SRAM and within the chip.
- The engines run concurrently, so a graph's floor is its busiest engine, not the sum of the engines.

**Why not prefill/decode.**

- LLM disaggregation separates a phase that reads its weights once per prompt (compute-bound) from one that
  re-reads the KV cache for every token (bandwidth-bound).
- Our stages alternate on every chunk for the whole stream. Both are far from both rooflines. Their interaction is
  through time and chip count, not through memory bandwidth.

One correction from our own measurements. An apparent 2x slowdown of the DiT when co-located with the decoder at 16
chips was a compiler-option leak from the decoder into the DiT's graphs. Fixed, the DiT runs at the same speed
co-located as alone (299 vs 300 ms). The coupling cost below is not interference in the LLM sense.

## 3 What bounds each stage

We extend the roofline to the classes that matter on a multi-engine accelerator. For every graph that carries the
latency (from a system trace of a warm run), we capture a device profile and compute one floor per resource:

- compute: FLOPs over peak;
- HBM: bytes over bandwidth;
- tensor-engine busy time;
- DMA busy time;
- serial time: only one engine busy, or idle.

**Table 1: the top graphs, 4-chip monolith, 480x768, ms per execution.**

| Stage | Graph | Time | Compute floor | HBM floor | Tensor busy | DMA busy | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DiT | attention block | 6.38 | 1.84 | 0.60 | 4.37 | 5.25 | on-chip movement |
| DiT | attention block | 6.56 | 1.84 | 0.64 | 4.21 | 5.31 | on-chip movement |
| DiT | MXFP8 graph | 23.95 | 3.81 | 3.89 | 12.99 | 20.89 | on-chip movement |
| VAE | halo convolution | 5.71 | 2.53 | 0.23 | 5.02 | 2.89 | array utilization |
| VAE | halo convolution | 5.47 | 2.48 | 0.33 | 4.96 | 3.27 | array utilization |

**The DiT is bound by on-chip data movement.**

- Its DMA queues are busy 82% of the time but move 8.8x what HBM traffic requires.
- The excess is movement between on-chip memories: MXFP8 activation transposes, dynamic gathers (about half of DMA
  time), and transfers averaging 157 KB.

**The decoder is bound by array utilization.**

- The tensor engine is busy about 90% of the time at 44-50% of the FLOPs it could deliver.
- The convolution shapes leave the systolic array half empty.

**Ceilings.** The per-chunk ceilings at bf16 peak are about 155 ms for the DiT and about 58 ms for the decoder,
against 552 and 128 ms measured. Different bounds mean different scaling.

**Table 2: per-stage scaling, 480x768, ms per chunk.**

| Chips | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DiT alone (period) | - | 1192 | 686 | 464 | 375 |
| Decoder pod | 324 | 213 | 173 | 153 | - |
| Decoder inline (on the DiT's chips) | - | - | 151 | 133 | 143 |

From 2 to 16 chips the DiT gains 3.2x. From 1 to 8 chips the decoder gains 2.1x, and inline it stops gaining after
4 chips.

## 4 The cost of coupling

T_i(n) is the time of stage i on one unit of work on n chips.

**Co-located on N chips:**

- P_co = T_d(N) + T_v(N)
- chip-seconds per unit: C_co = N (T_d(N) + T_v(N))

**Disaggregated, n_d DiT chips and n_v decoder chips:**

- P_dis = max(T_d(n_d), T_v(n_v)) + ε, where ε = 0.17 ms per 1.1 MB latent over EFA with NIXL.

**Rate matching.** One decoder replica serves k = T_d(n_d) / T_v(n_v) denoiser replicas. Beyond k, the operating
layer adds decoder replicas and spreads sessions over them. When T_v > T_d, a denoiser needs more decoder capacity:
bigger decoder replicas, or more of them for stateless decoders.

**Gain.** Throughput per chip improves by C_co / C_dis, from two terms:

- each stage runs at its own efficient chip count;
- neither stage's compute waits on the other's.

The per-stream speedup is bounded by 2x, at T_d = T_v.

**Validation.** Using only the per-stage measurements of Table 2:

- the model predicts all 15 measured LingBot configurations at 480x768 within 1.4% (3 co-located, 12
  disaggregated);
- at 720p it implies a 258 ms inline decoder at 16 chips, against 254 ms measured;
- it predicts 3.7 and 2.1 streams per 1-chip decoder for 2- and 4-chip DiTs; 3 and 2 run without slowdown
  (section 7.6).

**Extrapolation.**

- T_d(n) = a/n + b + c log2 n, fitted on 2, 4 and 8 chips, predicts 385 ms at 16 chips (375 measured, +2.5%).
- The decoder curve fitted on 1, 2 and 4 chips predicts 168 ms at 8 (153 measured, +10%). The error is on the side
  that understates disaggregation's gain.

**When it does not pay.**

- Splitting a fixed budget starves the stage that scales: LingBot at 8 chips runs 20.0 fps co-located against 17.5
  for 4 + x.
- Disaggregation pays when it adds chips the co-located replica cannot use, or frees chips the decoder wastes.

## 5 Role disaggregation as an operating-system abstraction

A pipeline is a set of **roles** (conditioner + denoiser, decoder) connected by **typed hand-offs** (a latent chunk
plus its metadata). The operating layer owns five decisions.

1. **Sizing:** chips per replica for each role, from its scaling curve.
2. **Replication and rate matching:** replicas per role, at a ratio from the cost model.
3. **Placement:** same node (NeuronLink) or across nodes (EFA). The hand-off is small enough that placement is nearly
   free.
4. **State:**
   - The denoiser's KV cache is per stream.
   - The decoder's causal cache is per session for LingBot, and none for SA3.
   - A decoder serving many streams swaps each session's cache in per chunk.
5. **Failure domains:** a decoder replica fails without taking its streams down. A failed write sets the decoder
   aside, and the next chunk goes to another.

**Sizing is a deploy-time decision.** Steps, resolution, window length and model are fixed per service before
serving, so T_d and T_v are known in advance. When a service mixes configurations, or the mix shifts, each role's
pool scales on its own. A co-located replica has a fixed ratio between its denoiser and decoder chips.

## 6 [SYSTEM]: design and implementation

**Resources.**

- Kubernetes DRA allocates Neuron devices, each paired with its own EFA through the device-group attributes.
- Two-device claims must take directly linked pairs (same topology_x); any other pair fails collective setup.
- Larger claims are count requests inside one device group. Per-device pairing constraints at 16 devices timed out
  the scheduler.

**Transport.**

- NIXL with the LIBFABRIC backend over EFA (FI_HMEM_NEURON) registers Neuron device memory by its runtime virtual
  address (nrt_tensor_get_va).
- Each session gets its own block of 4 receive slots on the decoder, and tags its notifications with the session id.
- A dead peer fails only the transfer in flight.

**Decoder role.**

- A decoder replica serves up to K sessions. Each session's causal cache is swapped in before its chunk and saved
  after; a saved cache is 427 MiB per rank.
- The replica reports the sessions it served recently (GET /load).

**Denoiser role.**

- Each new session links to the least-loaded Ready decoder found by pod discovery.
- A failed write sets that decoder aside for 60 s and retries the chunk on another.
- With no decoder available, the session is refused (503).

**Implementation.**

- The roles are serve.py (denoiser) and vae_serve.py (decoder); also wan/vae_pool.py, wan/nixl_link.py and the
  Kubernetes manifests.
- Coding agents were used to build and measure the system. The authors designed it and the experiments, and wrote
  this paper. [The authors must confirm this statement before submission.]

## 7 Evaluation

**Setup.**

- 2 trn3-dev1 nodes, 16 Trainium3 chips each; Neuron SDK 2.33; LNC2; MXFP8 DiT; bf16 decoder.
- Co-located and disaggregated configurations use the same model flags.
- One stream unless stated. Frame rate is measured at the client over 60 chunks.

**7.1 Equal-budget comparison (LingBot, 480x768).**

| Co-located | fps | Disaggregated (DiT + decoder chips) | fps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 chips | 14.3 | 2+1, 2+2 | 10.1 |
| 8 chips | 20.0 | 4+1, 4+2, 4+4 | 17.5 |
| 16 chips | 23.4 | 8+1, 8+2, 8+4, 8+8 | 25.5-26.0 |
| - | - | 16+1, 16+2, 16+4 | 32.1-32.2 |

- At 16 chips, 8+1 beats the co-located replica on 9 chips.
- 16+1 runs 1.37x faster than co-located 16, on one chip more.
- At 8 chips co-located wins (section 4).
- The tuned 2-chip DiT gives 14.7 fps at 2+1, against 14.3 for co-located 4. No tuned flags exist at 4 chips, so this
  cell is not like-for-like.

**7.2 Resolution and memory.** At 720p:

- the co-located 8-chip replica does not fit in device memory once the decoder loads, while the 8-chip DiT alone fits
  (8.5 fps);
- 4 chips fit neither way;
- at 16 chips, 16+1 runs 1.27x faster (12.7 vs 10.0 fps).

The decoder's share is smaller at 720p (about 33% vs 48% at 480), because attention grows with the square of the
tokens (10,560 per chunk vs 4,320). At high resolution, disaggregation decides whether a chip budget can serve the
model at all.

**7.3 Moving the stage ratio (Stable Audio 3, 2-chip DiT, 1800 s of audio).**

| Steps | Co-located | 2 + 1 decoder chip | 2 + 2 decoder chips |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 27.81 s | 27.02 s | 26.80 s |
| 25 | 14.88 s | 14.39 s | 14.16 s |
| 8 | 6.15 s | 5.48 s | 5.37 s |
| 4 | 4.07 s | 3.89 s | 3.32 s |
| 2 | 3.06 s | 3.75 s | 2.51 s |

- With many steps the DiT dominates, and the gain is small.
- With few steps the decoder dominates, and the gain needs decoder capacity: at 2 steps a 1-chip decoder loses, and a
  2-chip decoder wins 1.22x.
- Distillation is reducing step counts, which makes the decoder a larger share.
- LingBot at 2 steps: **[open, running]**. LingBot is distilled to 4 steps and cannot run more.

**7.4 Rate matching and horizontal scaling (LingBot).**

| Setup | Streams | fps per stream |
|---|---|---|
| One 1-chip decoder, 2-chip DiTs | 1 / 2 / 3 | 10.07 / 10.06 / 10.08 |
| One 1-chip decoder, 4-chip DiTs | 1 / 2 | 17.52 / 17.53 |
| 2 decoders (placed 3 + 3) | 6 | 10.01-10.05 |
| 3 decoders (placed 3 + 3 + 3) | 9 | 10.00-10.07 |

- A stream's frames are byte-identical whether it runs alone or shares its decoder. Only the decode latency grows,
  through queueing: 486 → 723 → 833 ms.
- Four sessions' caches take 1.7 GiB, and the decoder rank uses about 3 GiB of device memory in total.
- Stable Audio at 25 steps: one decoder feeds 4 DiT jobs at almost full speed (14.13 → 14.44 s), and saturates at 6
  (about 20.1 s). The model predicted about 4.5.

**7.5 Streams per chip and cost [open].** Streams per chip at a 16 fps target, and cost per stream-hour at on-demand
prices, from the measured cells. No new runs are needed.

**7.6 Latency (press-to-photon, LingBot 480, 100 presses).**

| Setup | fps | Median | p90 | Decode of the first frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Co-located, 4 chips | 15.5 | 1114 ms | 1416 ms | 62 ms |
| 4 + 1 decoder chip | 17.5 | 1221 ms | 1471 ms | 314 ms |
| 4 + 4 decoder chips | 17.6 | 1114 ms | 1371 ms | 209 ms |

Decoder chips buy latency, not throughput. A decoder sized for latency matches the co-located median, beats its p90,
and keeps the throughput gain.

**7.7 Is it the decoupling or the pods?** The DiT and decoder can run in two containers of one pod, on disjoint
chips, with NIXL inside the pod. They give 17.51 fps at 4+1 and 25.74 at 8+1, the same as across pods (17.54,
25.76). The gain comes from decoupling the stages. Separate pods cost nothing, and add per-role scaling, sharing and
placement.

**7.8 Resilience.**

- A decoder pod deleted, or its device tainted as faulty, in the middle of a stream: about 3 s to switch, 140 of 140
  chunks delivered, no pause longer than 1.5 s.
- With no decoder available, the session is refused (503).
- Elasticity, adding decoder pods under load: **[open]**.
- Same-node vs cross-node placement: **[open]**.

**7.9 Single-replica optimization.** Moving JPEG encoding off the critical path raised the co-located 4-chip
replica from 14.2 to 15.5 fps, with byte-identical frames. It applies to every configuration and does not change the
comparison.

## 8 Lessons

- **Profile the binding resource, not compute versus memory.** Neither stage sits on its roofline, and the reason
  differs per stage.
- **Check that an apparent interference is real.** Ours was a process-wide compiler setting.
- **Device topology matters to DRA claims.** Linked pairs and device groups must be part of the claim.
- **A shared claim gives two containers mismatched device orders.** Give each role its own claim.
- **Measurement tools have ceilings.** A probe computing image metrics on every frame capped at about 22 fps.

## 10 Conclusion

Two-stage generative pipelines are limited by the coupling of two stages with different bounds, not by either stage
alone. Treating the stages as independently sized, replicated and placed roles, an operating-system responsibility,
recovers the lost chip time. A cost model the operator can compute at deploy time predicts the right sizing.
